01 · Cyberian Culture

Applied cybernetics, lived as a social scene

It isn't a game and it isn't a think tank. It's a tribe of creators and creatives who value production and want to build a better future, and who treat their own culture-making as a live experiment.

Production over consumption

We are streamers, writers, artists, builders and organizers. The common thread is that we make things. Watching is fine. Making is the point.

Every meetup is an experiment

Each gathering picks one thing to make and spread: a meme, a bit, a piece of work, an experiment. Members carry it out for two weeks and we see how it travels.

The research stays quiet

A neutral research layer logs, tracks and reports on the club itself. It doesn't steer. The group studies its own cycle of intelligence while running it.

Decentralized by design

Anyone can start a Cyberian Club. There is no head office to ask permission from. The whole thing is built to outgrow its founders.

Shared success

The old clubs worked because members lifted each other: jobs, introductions, collaborations, opportunities. Cyberians play for the tribe, and wins are shared.

A civilizational drive

The long aim is a futuristic new culture with ambition built in. Discernment in every medium, from great books to great streams. No timidity, no apathy.

Against the surrogate life

Greatness, made collective

Most people want more than they have: to be stronger, sharper, more cultured, more successful. Most also spend their days on stand-ins for those things. Games stand in for winning. Feeds stand in for friendship. Watching stands in for doing.

Cyberian culture is a way out of the stand-ins. It asks you to take the real swing, in public, with other people, and to accept that striving for excellence means you will sometimes fail. Failure is data. The tribe learns from it.

Greatness here isn't a club for the gifted. It's what happens when a network of ordinary, ambitious people stops spectating and starts producing together.

02 · Become a Cyberian Adventurer

From receiver to source

Becoming a Cyberian adventurer means you stop being a passive receiver in the tee and become a deliberate source. You step out from behind the screen and into the arena.

It's part guild membership, part field research, part frontier settling. You make things for the tribe, not only for yourself. You treat everything you create as a probe into how collective intelligence forms. And you go somewhere new with it.

PATH / STREAM

Go live

Broadcast the meetups, the experiments and your own work. Cross-pollinate with other streamers in the club.

PATH / BUILD

Make the thing

Write it, design it, code it, film it. Bring a creation to the meetup and let the tribe carry it out.

PATH / GUARD

Join the Guard

Represent the tribe at streamer events and competitions, like Safe Zone. Play to win, and share the prize.

PATH / OUTPOST

Found an outpost

Start a Cyberian Club where you live. No charter needed. See Clubs & Outposts for the kit.

The adventurer's code

Five commitments

  1. Be a sourcePut something into the world every cycle, however small.
  2. Play for the tribeYour win is the club's win. Lift the people next to you.
  3. Treat it as a probeEverything you make is also a question about how culture moves. Pay attention to the answer.
  4. Welcome failureStriving means missing sometimes. A flop that gets logged teaches the plur more than a safe bet.
  5. Leave the screenShow up in person. The arena is a room full of people, not a comment section.

03 · The Method

Make it, spread it, see what happens

ACCRU runs on a two-week cycle. Each meetup launches one thing. The next meetup reports on where it went.

D012345678910111213D14
Meetup: launch + report Members propagate Log check-in
Day 0

Pick

The meetup chooses one thing to make and spread: a meme, a bit, an experiment. The session is streamed.

Days 1–13

Propagate

Members carry it into their own channels, streams and circles, each in their own way.

Throughout

Log

The research layer records where it showed up, who picked it up, and how it changed along the way.

Day 14

Report

The next meetup opens with what happened. Then it picks the next thing.

Why it works this way

The plur studies itself

Every intelligent system runs a loop: it observes the world, reacts internally, and interacts back. A club is no different. The two-week cycle is that loop made visible. We observe how our creation lands, react as a group, and interact with the next round.

The research layer is neutral. It records and reports. It does not run campaigns, pick winners, or steer members. What we learn about how ideas move from sources to receivers comes from watching ourselves honestly.

Everyone at a meetup knows it is streamed and logged. Being open about the research is part of the culture.

04 · Clubs & Outposts

Outposts get built, not chartered

ACCRU is the Austin club. Anywhere else, anyone can start a Cyberian Club. You don't need permission. You need a few people and a rhythm.

The outpost kit
1

A place

A living room, a bar back room, a studio. Somewhere you can make noise and set up a camera.

2×

A rhythm

Twice a month. Often enough to keep momentum, spaced enough for two weeks of propagation.

1

A stream

Broadcast every meetup. The stream is how the outpost talks to the rest of the network.

1

A thing to spread

Leave every meetup with one creation to carry out. Report back next time.

How it fits together

The Cyberian Institute

The Institute is the planned home for the research and the network. ACCRU is its flagship club and research unit in Austin. Cyberian Clubs is the wider network of outposts.

Outposts aren't official chapters. They run themselves. The Institute's job is to support them: sharing what the research learns, connecting outposts to each other, and, as it grows, helping fund clubs that are doing good work.

The first big gathering of outposts is planned as an Outpost Building summit.
